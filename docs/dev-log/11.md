Welcome to another edition of our Dev Log where we share how the development of Flipper One is going. This Log focuses on major improvements to the design of the main board.

---

## Enforcing a uniform size of test points

The development of Flipper One involves rigorous testing, and this includes bed-of-nails testing of assembled boards. This means that we have to produce test fixtures containing spring-loaded pins whose position maps to pre-defined test points on a board. During testing, a fixture presses a board onto the pins, and if even one tip of a pin misses its designated test point, the accuracy of a test run is compromised.

![Comparison of tip placement on old and new test points](files/pics/dev-log/11/dev-11-testPoints.png "Comparison of tip placement on old and new test points")

To remedy this, we've decided to set the diameter of all test points on the main board to 1.2mm. As a result, there is now more margin for pins to safely connect to test points, and it's easier for us to produce accurate test fixtures.

## Improved positioning of components on the bottom of the board

The main board of Flipper One contains two Ethernet connectors, both of which have to be soldered to the board. Previously, there were other components placed in close proximity to one of these connectors on the bottom side of the board. This meant that we couldn't produce wave soldering fixtures as there just wasn't enough space for the pallet material to protect components located close to the connector from molten solder.

:::hint{type="info"}

When a circuit board is made, some components placed on its top side may have wires that must be inserted into special copper-lined holes. During the process known as wave soldering, the board is placed onto a special pallet called the soldering fixture. 

The fixture contains openings exposing the copper-lined holes, while other parts of the board are protected by the pallet material. The bottom of the board is then passed over molten solder, ensuring that the previously inserted wires are soldered inside the holes, and components are fixed in one place.

:::

This is no longer the case: we've improved the design by freeing up space around that connector. With this change, we can manufacture soldering fixtures for the main board.

![Component placement comparison relative to RJ45](files/pics/dev-log/11/dev-11-rj45.png "Component placement comparison relative to RJ45")

## Separating the USB-A connector from other components

At the top of its frame, Flipper One has a USB-A port, and the main board needs to be protected from electrostatic discharge entering through this port. This is usually done by placing a separate protection device on the board. However, both the USB-A connector and the device are soldered at the same time in the same oven. If they are placed too close to each other, solder paste on two neighbouring pads can form a "bridge", causing a short circuit and potentially damaging the components when the board is used.

In the latest iteration of the main board, we've avoided this problem by moving the protection device to the other side of the board. We can now keep on soldering with fewer defects, bringing Flipper One even closer to release.

![Changes to the placement of the discharge protection element](files/pics/dev-log/11/dev-11-usb.png "Changes to the placement of the discharge protection element")

## Avoiding soldering problems with the HDMI connector

Some connectors have a special part called a locating pin. When a connector is placed onto a board, this pin fits into a hole and ensures that the connector stays in its position and its pins touch the pads on the board.

To avoid the same soldering issues that faced the USB-A connector, we've moved small components so they're more than 1.2mm away from the HDMI connector's locating pin. The benefit is also the same: soldering is now more reliable.

![An illustration of component placement close to the HDMI connector](files/pics/dev-log/11/dev-11-hdmi.png "An illustration of component placement close to the HDMI connector")
